2. The Service, in preview, as is

ProofStream is a record-keeping tool. In the current preview you can:

  • create a project in a discipline (music, design, writing, film or software);
  • fingerprint a file in your browser with SHA-256, so only the fingerprint and the details you enter are saved, with a time set by ProofStream’s server;
  • build and export a timeline of versions;
  • record an ISRC you already have, with splits, roles and territories, checked to add up to 100%;
  • request verification of a recording claim, with evidence fingerprinted on your device;
  • where a claim is verified by a ProofStream administrator, have a hash of that claim anchored on a public blockchain.

The Service is offered in preview, as is, and as available. Features may change, break, or be withdrawn. We do not promise that the preview will be uninterrupted, error-free, or kept in its current form.

3. What ProofStream does not do

To be clear, ProofStream:

  • does not upload your files. Only fingerprints and the details you enter are saved;
  • does not issue ISRCs. SoundExchange and IFPI-authorised agencies do. ProofStream only records the ISRCs you already have;
  • does not treat a fingerprint or a timestamp as proof of legal ownership or copyright;
  • does not register copyright, provide legal advice, or decide disputes about ownership, authorship or rights;
  • does not collect royalties or take payments;
  • does not, in this preview, run a label or catalog workflow. That track is not available yet.

While ProofStream is in preview, on-chain anchoring runs on Ethereum’s Sepolia test network, not on Ethereum mainnet. A test network is public and experimental. Records on Sepolia are not the same as records on mainnet. We may use a different network later.

6. ISRCs, splits and verification

ProofStream does not issue, generate, assign or sell ISRCs, and it does not collect or pay royalties. If you record an ISRC or a split, you are responsible for obtaining that ISRC from an authorised agency and for the accuracy of the data you enter, including that splits add up to 100% for the roles and territories you list.

You may ask ProofStream to verify a claim, typically with hashes of a split sheet or distributor statement. Evidence is fingerprinted on your device and is not uploaded. Verification is reviewed by a ProofStream administrator and may be accepted, refused, or delayed. We do not promise that any request will be verified. If you do not want a claim anchored on a public blockchain, do not request verification.

If more than one person claims the same ISRC or audio, we may mark the claims as contested. Other users are not shown your email address or account details. A contested mark is not a decision about who is right.

7. Blockchain records

When a ProofStream administrator verifies a version of a recording claim, the Service may write a cryptographic hash of that claim’s canonical record to a public blockchain smart contract (the ProofStreamRegistry). Blockchain records are public, permanent, and replicated by independent parties. Once a hash is anchored, neither we nor anyone else can change or delete it.

Anchors contain hashes, not your name or email. Anyone who holds the matching canonical record may be able to confirm it against the anchor. During preview, anchoring runs on Ethereum’s Sepolia test network (see Section 3).

Public blockchain networks and RPC providers receive the hashes and transactions needed to write an anchor. Those networks are outside our control.
